A Few Thoughts About the Russian Cultural Garden

Over the past few weeks, many people have asked me what has been happening at the Russian Cultural Garden.

As one of the co-founders of the Russian Cultural Garden and the first President of the Russian Cultural Gardens nonprofit organization, I feel it is important to share a few thoughts as we begin a new chapter in the Garden's history.

For the past two years, the Russian Cultural Garden has been led by a wonderful team of dedicated volunteers. Through their energy, creativity, and commitment, many memorable events were organized and many worthwhile ideas became reality. I am sincerely grateful to each of them for their service and contribution.

Recently, this team concluded its work and transferred the Garden's records, projects, and responsibilities to the current Board of Directors. Over time, it became clear that we had different visions for the Garden's future, and each of us has chosen a different path. Such transitions are a natural part of the life of nonprofit organizations, and I respect that.

But this experience has led me to reflect on something much deeper.

There are some things that do not depend on titles, positions, or official roles.

You can love your Garden as a volunteer. You can love it as a visitor. You can simply stop by from time to time. Or you can support it from afar—with encouragement, ideas, donations, or special projects. The Yuri Gagarin monument, for example, became a reality thanks to the generosity and dedication of people from different countries, different states, and different communities who believed in this Garden.

A genuine connection to your Garden does not depend on serving on its Board of Directors.

It simply remains.

There is another thought that feels especially important to me.

The history of the Russian Cultural Garden has long become part of the history of Cleveland. It belongs not to one individual or one generation, but to everyone who has invested their time, talent, labor, ideas, resources, and a part of their heart into its growth—everyone who has ever considered this Garden their own.

One of the remarkable qualities of public institutions is that history never erases those who helped build them.

Everyone who has contributed to the Russian Cultural Garden will always remain part of its story. It does not matter when someone joined, how long they served, or what position they held. Their contribution has become part of something larger—the cultural heritage of our city.

That is why I hope our love for the Garden does not end when our official responsibilities do. The Garden remains. Its story continues. And everyone who has ever called it their own will forever remain part of that story.

Today, I simply want to say one word.

Thank you.

Thank you to every volunteer, Board member, donor, artist, supporter, visitor, and friend who has helped make the Russian Cultural Garden the place that thousands of Cleveland residents and visitors know and cherish today.

It is a privilege to be part of this story—together with everyone who helped build it yesterday, everyone who cares for it today, and everyone who will carry it forward tomorrow.

The story of the Russian Cultural Garden continues. And perhaps that is what matters most.

Svetlana Stolyarova Co-founder of the Russian Cultural Garden and first President of the Russian Cultural Gardens nonprofit organization Approved by the Founders and the Board of the Russian Cultural Gardens non-profit organization
